Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Seattle Homeowners Should Know

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

What It Looks Like

  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
  • One wall noticeably cooler under your hand than the rest
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • Baseboards that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Damp, stale odor concentrated along a single wall
  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Insulation Holding Water

Fiberglass batts soak up water and hold it against the studs. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Moisture in Exterior Walls

Water in a perimeter wall may reach the sheathing on the far side of the cavity. It gets a moisture reading prior to closing, since it holds dampness a long time.

Growth Behind the Paint

Mold thrives on the rear face of drywall because it is paper covered, dark and has no moving air. That side may be coated in growth even as the room side appears spotless.

Water Next Door

It slips beneath the bottom plate and reaches the closet or room on the other side. We check both sides of every affected wall and the flooring along it.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Starts Climbing

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.

Our Process

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.
