Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Seattle Restoration Co provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Seattle, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.
