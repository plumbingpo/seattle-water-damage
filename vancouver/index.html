What to Do Before Our Vancouver Crew Arrives

What you do before help reaches your Vancouver address has a real effect on how the job goes. Safety comes before saving anything. Keep out of water that touches sockets, appliances or wiring, and only cut the power if the breaker box is in a dry place you can get to safely. If the water is from a sewer or toilet backup, do not touch it at all.

Then shut the water off. Close the valve at the fixture if there is one, or the main shutoff for the house. Then move what you can carry: rugs, electronics, papers, and small furniture go to a dry room. Put foil or plastic under the legs of heavy furniture so the wood does not stain the carpet.

Before mopping, record the damage on your phone, since an insurance company will ask what it looked like at the start. Mop or towel up small amounts of clean water if it is safe, and leave the rest to the crew heading to Vancouver.

Where Water Damage Starts in Vancouver Properties

Ask anyone on our crew what fills the schedule in Vancouver and you will hear the same six answers. None of them is rare, and each gets worse the longer the water sits.

Mold: Most mold growth is the follow-up to an earlier leak that seemed to be taken care of. Surfaces dry with towels and a box fan, yet the pad, subfloor and inside of the wall hold moisture. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours, then spread quietly until you smell it or see spotting.

Hoses: Every sink, toilet and washer is fed by a flexible connector that holds full house pressure around the clock. When one splits, it sprays until the water is turned off, which can mean hours if nobody is home. Replacing old connectors and knowing where your shutoff sits are the cheapest protection available.

Pumps: Sump pumps, where a home has one, tend to fail at the worst moment after years of running without attention. What we usually see is a jammed float, a motor that has worn out or a breaker that tripped. The result is several inches of water over the lowest floor, soaking stored boxes, flooring and the bottom of the walls. A battery backup and a quick test before the rainy part of the year go a long way.

Toilets: Among bathroom emergencies in Vancouver, the toilet that will not stop rising tops the list. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. Either way it moves under tile edges and vanity cabinets, so the floor needs to be checked with a meter.

Cooling: Few people think about the small pipe that carries condensation away from an AC system. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. The leak is gradual, so drywall and insulation may be soaked long before you spot a mark. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. It reaches ground-level rooms, garages and walkout lower levels on the downhill side before anything else. Our Seattle Restoration Co crews extract it, remove what cannot be cleaned and dry the structure to verified readings.
