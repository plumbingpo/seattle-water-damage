Drying a home is only half the job. Even with normal moisture readings, you are left with opened walls, flooring that is gone and exposed subfloor. Rebuilding all of that is what water damage repair means. Our team in Seattle replaces what could not be saved and matches it to what was there before, so the room looks like a room again and not a job site.

No two repair jobs are the same size. A minor loss can need just a couple of feet of wallboard plus baseboard and a coat of paint. On a large one it can mean insulation, full sheets of drywall, subfloor, hardwood or laminate, cabinet boxes, trim and doors. Our aim is a like-for-like replacement, and when a true match cannot be found we say so directly and let you decide.

We do not close anything in while it is still damp. Ahead of installing anything new, our team meters the framing and subfloor to verify normal moisture. Mold hidden in a newly painted wall usually traces back to someone covering damp lumber. We also give you a written scope and a schedule before work starts, so you know what is being rebuilt and in what order.

What a Professional Brings to Water Damage Repair

Repair after water damage is not ordinary remodeling. A rebuilder must know the path the water took, the harm it caused and if the structure hidden by the wall is actually dry. Our team works from the moisture records taken during drying, so we know which studs were wet, which subfloor was treated and where to look twice. Knowing the background stops issues from being hidden behind new wallboard and a coat of paint.

Then there is the craft of making things match. You will see a visible patch every single day. Our finishers blend the texture on walls, carry paint to a natural stopping point and, where it exists, find flooring that aligns with your current boards. Should a match prove impossible, we present choices like replacing the floor up to a doorway. Our team pulls any permits the job needs and sends updates to your insurance company.

Warning Signs for Water Damage Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Cabinet bases or side panels are swollen, soft or peeling at the bottom.
  • Seams are opening, or edges are curling and swelling, on vinyl plank or laminate.
  • Following water from above, part of the ceiling droops, has cracks or crumbles when touched.
  • Water under the tile left cracked grout lines or tiles that rock when stepped on.
  • Since the water damage, certain doors drag on the jamb or miss the latch.
  • Where water ran down a wall, the paint has blistered, peeled or turned brown.
  • Trim around doors and along the floor is bowed and will not lie flat.

How to Prevent Water Damage Repair Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Once a year, renew the caulk at sinks, showers and tubs to keep water from entering the wall.
  • Hold off on heavy traffic and wet cleaning until new adhesives, caulk and paint have cured.
  • Completed-repair photos and the scope document belong in your home records.
  • Save spare tile and flooring, plus paint color details, for matching later repairs.
  • No wall or floor should be sealed by any contractor until you have seen final moisture readings.
  • Run the bath fan during showers and for a while afterward so rebuilt walls stay dry.
