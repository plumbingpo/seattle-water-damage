No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for Seattle Restoration Co know it well. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks
  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials

Why Structural Drying Is Not a DIY Job

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

How Our Structural Drying Works

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Mapping the Moisture

Using moisture meters, thermal imaging and hygrometers, we create a comprehensive moisture map of all affected areas. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline readings.

2

Setting the Equipment

Commercial LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are strategically positioned based on our moisture map. We tune airflow and temperature to suit the materials that need drying.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

A technician comes back every day, reads moisture at each monitoring point, moves equipment as needed and logs the progress. You receive updates on the drying timeline.

4

Dry Standard Verification

Once every affected material reads at an acceptable moisture level, we record the final numbers, pull the equipment and certify the property as meeting dry standard.

Signs You Need Structural Drying in Seattle

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Trapped moisture often announces itself with a persistent musty odor after cleanup
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Window glass that fogs up near the affected rooms
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining
